# Database audit — 2026-10-01

Audit of the whole new intranet database (`sql/00` – `sql/13`, 15 schemas) on
four questions: is anything stored twice, is every table normalised as far as
it can be, is it DPDP-compliant, and do the documents describe the database as
it actually is.

Everything below was checked against a fresh build of the SQL files on
PostgreSQL 17, not against the documents.

| Measured on the final build | |
|---|---|
| Tables | 546 (183 `ref` lookups + 363) |
| Views · functions | 132 · 163 |
| Personal-data columns in the inventory | 185, on 112 tables |
| Tables under the audit trigger | 523 of 546 |
| Tests | `99` 677 · `98` 319 · `97` 327 — all pass; scripts 90 and 91 apply |

## 1. Result at a glance

| Question | Result |
|---|---|
| Duplication | 5 cases of a fact stored twice were found and removed or locked (§3). The rest of what looks like duplication is deliberate and listed with its reason (§4). |
| Normalisation | No arrays, JSON, enums or free-text status columns anywhere. Every table has a primary key. Two repeating-group cases remain by decision, as standard (§7). |
| DPDP | 18 free-text columns about people were missing from the personal-data inventory, and the reporting role could read 10 tables carrying such text. Both fixed (§5). Structural controls were already sound. |
| Docs vs database | 1 stale figure, 1 document using names that were never built, 1 document missing from the index, a few card texts. All corrected; generated documents regenerated (§6). |

## 3. Duplication and normalisation — fixed

| # | Where | What was wrong | Now |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `events.club_point_award` | stored the club beside the event, although the event's proposal already says who organised it | column dropped; `v_club_points` reads the club through the event |
| 2 | `events.event_stall` | a stall created from an approved application copied its name, type, category, size, owner and needs from the application | an applied stall holds only the link, its location and its active flag (enforced by `CHECK`); `v_stall` shows applied and organisers' stalls alike |
| 3 | `facility.booking_group` | repeated the requester, title and event of its booking requests, with nothing keeping them equal | the group is only its reference number; `v_booking_summary` reads the rest from the requests |
| 4 | `wf.approval_instance` | carries the document type as well as the chain, and the chain already has a document type | kept (read constantly), but tied by one composite foreign key — the two cannot disagree |
| 5 | `events.proposal_speaker`, `events.proposal_visibility_scope` | the same person or the same scope could be listed twice on a proposal | unique indexes |
| 6 | `tms.ticket_notice_rule` | no primary key | `id` added (by the helpdesk session) |

Each fix has a test: the refusal fires, or the derived value is still right.

## 4. What looks like duplication and is deliberate

| Pattern | Examples | Why it stays |
|---|---|---|
| Snapshot at a decision | `wf.approval_step` (the chain as it was at submission), `wf.approval_instance` override settings, `exam.course_result.total_pct`, the discount on `events.coupon_redemption`, points on `club_point_award` | the value must not move when the rule changes afterwards; a published result or a round in flight is a record |
| Ledger | `facility.room_hold`, `asset_hold` (room and buffers repeated from their source) | one table carries the no-double-booking `EXCLUDE` constraint for timetable, bookings, stays and maintenance alike; rows are written only by triggers |
| Cross-module copy | `facility.event_request` holds the dates and headcount of an `events.event_proposal` | facility must work without the events module; the copy is frozen once submitted; an approved change opens a new package and releases the old one |
| Constraint carrier | `events.event_team.event_id`, the owning org unit on documents under approval | needed inside a unique or exclusion constraint; a trigger keeps it equal to its source |
| Counter that is the fact itself | `iam.otp_challenge.attempt_count`, `iam.mfa_webauthn.sign_count`, `events.event_mail_recipient.open_count`, `tms.ticket_number_counter.last_no` | there are no rows to count instead: failed tries, the authenticator's own counter, mail opens and the last number issued are recorded as numbers |
| Two links to the same table in different roles | requester and approver, employee and external person, setter and moderator | different facts, not one fact twice — this is most of the 138 pairs |
| Surrogate key only | 48 tables: logs and events, free budget or expense lines, one-row settings, tables whose uniqueness is an `EXCLUDE` or a trigger | a natural key would be wrong (two identical expense lines are legitimate) |
| Unregistered free text | resolutions, programme and outcome statements, policies, descriptions of events, rooms and categories, article bodies | about the institution or configuration, not about a person |
| Erasable ids readable by reporting | `academic.elective_preference`, `academic.feedback_submission`, `facility.vendor` | ids only, no names or text; reporting cannot resolve them (no access to `people.person` or `pii`) |

## 5. DPDP

**Already in place and confirmed:** `pii` isolated in its own schema; one
inventory (`privacy.data_element`) driving erasure, export and audit
redaction; purposes with legal basis and retention; consent and its
withdrawal; guardian consent for minors; data-principal requests, breach
register, processors; append-only audit with erasable values redacted;
reporting role without identities; secrets never stored in clear.

**Fixed**

1. *Remarks about people were outside the inventory.* 18 columns registered,
   so they are now covered by erasure, export and audit redaction:

   | Column | Category · on erasure |
   |---|---|
   | `academic.faculty_unavailability.reason` | HEALTH · anonymise |
   | `academic.faculty_course_preference.remarks` | EMPLOYMENT · anonymise |
   | `academic.credit_overload_approval.reason`, `exam.certificate.cancel_reason`, `exam.script_mark.note`, `research.viva.remarks`, `events.event_certificate.revoke_reason` | EDUCATION · retained (academic record) |
   | `privacy.data_principal_request.details`, `privacy.dpr_request_event.note`, `privacy.breach_incident.description` | IDENTITY · retained (compliance record) |
   | `wf.approval_instance.note`, `wf.approval_action.note`, `authz.account_role.reason`, `authz.account_permission.reason` | IDENTITY · retained (decision record) |
   | `wf.approval_comment.body`, `.suggested_value`, `events.event_sponsor.name` | IDENTITY · anonymise |
   | `iam.user_device.name` | DEVICE_NETWORK · delete |

2. *The reporting role could read tables with such text.* `SELECT` revoked
   from `intranet_analytics` on `exam.attendance_condonation`,
   `academic.faculty_unavailability`, `academic.faculty_course_preference`,
   `wf.approval_delegation`, `facility.booking_request`,
   `facility.event_request`, `events.club_change_request`,
   `events.event_change_request`, `events.event_stall`,
   `events.event_sponsor`. The summary views stay readable.

**Not something the database can settle** — the POLICY items in
`docs/dpdp-compliance.md` (retention periods, notice wording, who the DPO
is, processor contracts) still need the DPO / legal sign-off. The categories
and erasure actions chosen in the table above are a technical judgement and
belong on that list. The application layer (consent screens, export, the
erasure job) is not built yet.

## 7. Decisions (taken 2026-10-01 on standard university practice)

| # | Question | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `academic.attainment_policy` has `level1_pct`, `level2_pct`, `level3_pct`; `pii.address` has `line1`, `line2` — repeating groups in the strict sense. | **Kept.** Three attainment levels are what the NBA / NAAC outcome-based method uses, and two address lines are the postal standard; a child table would add joins and no safety. |
| 2 | Every approval chain currently lets a higher level approve out of turn (never below two levels) — including curriculum sign-off and marks revision. It is one switch per chain (`allows_level_override`). | **Statutory and financial chains strict, the rest keep the override.** `CURRICULUM` and `INTERNAL_MARKS_REVISION` (each statutory body is a required step) and `EVENT_SETTLEMENT` (verify, then sanction) are seeded with `allows_level_override = false`; bookings, stays, trips, events and clubs keep it. Data only — an administrator can switch any chain. Tested in `99` and `97`. |
| 3 | `facility.vendor.name` is unregistered (treated as a firm); `events.event_sponsor.name` is registered (a sponsor may be an individual). | **Registered.** A sole proprietor's name and GSTIN (which embeds the PAN) identify a person: both are now in the inventory as `IDENTITY`, `RETAIN_LEGAL` (purchase records are kept for the tax-law period). The GSTIN stays in clear because it is a public registration printed on invoices. |
| 4 | `facility.maintenance_schedule.last_done_on` and `academic.class_session_topic.topic` (free text beside the lesson-plan link) are partly derivable. | **Kept:** the first is kept by trigger for scheduling, the second records what was actually taught when it departs from the plan. |

## 8. Limits of this audit

- Structural duplication is found by query; a fact repeated under a different
  name is found only by reading. The events, approval-engine and facility
  schemas were read through; academic, exam, research and the helpdesk were
  checked by query and by judging every flagged case.
- The document check covers every *name and number*. Sentences describing
  behaviour were verified where the audit touched them, not line by line in
  every document.
- DPDP compliance is shown at the level of the database design. It is not a
  legal opinion.
